# Communes of the new wilayas 59 to 69: to review

**For:** the owner. **What to do:** read the tables and "To settle", then tick the box at the end.

Until you approve this file, the app refuses these 108 communes: they are greyed out in the
lists, and old client records that name one of them keep an empty commune. A client can still
be given a wilaya from 59 to 69 without a commune.

## Result

- All 108 communes are in the same wilaya in our data as in the official law, under the same
  French and Arabic names. No difference was found.
- A second, independent list gives the same new wilaya and the same old wilaya for all 108.
- 3 points about dairas are still open (see "To settle"). None of them is guessed.

## How it was checked

1. **French:** Journal officiel (JO) 2026 no. 25, French edition, law 26-06 of 4 April 2026,
   articles 52 bis 10 to 52 bis 20. The file's fingerprint (SHA-256) is the one recorded by
   the data's author. The lists were extracted from the file and compared by program.
   The comparison ignored accents, capitals, spaces, apostrophes, hyphens and other signs.
2. **Arabic:** the Arabic edition of the same JO, same fingerprint test. 105 names were found
   in its numbered lists by program (ignoring spaces, hamza and other marks, and the usual
   final letter variants). 3 lines came out broken from the file and were compared by eye in
   the extracted text. The French comparison is the one that checks which wilaya each name is
   in; the Arabic one checks that the name is printed in the law's lists.
3. **Old wilaya:** the law does not print the old wilaya next to each commune. It is the one
   in the source data, which agrees with the first two digits of the commune's ONS code and
   with the second list (github.com/oussamidat/algerie-decoupage-administratif, licence
   ODbL, version 3f3c5d0). That list was only used to compare; nothing from it is in the app.
   The same law also rewrites the lists of the 10 old wilayas (03, 05, 07, 12, 13, 14, 17,
   26, 28 and 32). Their French lists match our data exactly, without the communes that left.

**Data source:** github.com/syscli/algeria, version 7789ea9, MIT licence.

## About dairas

The dairas come from executive decree 91-306 of 1991. **They may have changed since.** They
were not checked against any later text. A daira's Arabic name is the Arabic name of the
commune it is named after, only when there is exactly one such commune.

Because the new wilayas split some 1991 dairas, the app shows a daira once in each wilaya that
holds some of its communes. This concerns these daira names:

- Sebdou: wilayas 13 Tlemcen, 63 El Aricha

## Other checks, and what was not checked

- **Number of communes per wilaya, all 69:**
  - 21 wilayas (the 11 new and the 10 old ones above): full French lists match the 2026 law.
  - 17 wilayas (01, 08, 11, 30, 33, 39, 47 and 49 to 58): full French lists match law 19-12
    (JO 2019 no. 78), under our name or a recorded other spelling. One line without its dot
    ("5 In Amguel") was read by eye.
  - The other 31: only the **number** was compared, with the number written in law 84-09
    (JO 1984 no. 6). That JO is a scan, read by text recognition. It agrees, once the 24
    communes that ordinance 97-14 moved to Algiers are counted (6 from Boumerdès, 14 from
    Tipaza, 4 from Blida, also read by text recognition), with one exception: Mascara (29)
    has 47 communes today and 46 in 1984. The data's author reports this too: the text that
    split "Guettena El Mamounia" into two communes has not been found.
- The commune **names** of those 31 wilayas were not compared with the Journal officiel.
- Decree 26-206 (names, numbers and seats of wilayas 59 to 69) was not downloaded or checked.
- The second list writes 19 of the 108 Arabic names differently. Our spelling is the one in
  the Arabic edition of the law in all 19 cases.
